### Overall Evaluation

**1. Learning and working environment**

During the program, I experienced a learning environment that was professional but still welcoming. Senior members and participants in the same cohort were open to discussion whenever my team encountered difficulties with architecture design or AWS services. Timely feedback helped me identify unsuitable configurations earlier, avoid unnecessary rework, and reduce avoidable AWS costs.

The comfortable workspace and the learning spirit of other participants also motivated me to focus more seriously on the project. One improvement would be to arrange a more consistent in-person schedule for each team. Meeting regularly would make it easier for members to get to know each other, exchange ideas, and solve integration issues than relying mainly on online communication.

**2. Mentor and team-admin support**

What I appreciated most was the dedication of the mentors and team admins. During periods close to the deadline, the team still received feedback that helped us adjust the architecture, add supporting evidence, and correct inaccurate statements in the workshop. The mentors also shared practical experience and pointed out risks that beginners working with AWS can easily overlook.

I particularly valued their approach to guiding us toward an answer. Instead of providing a complete solution, they often suggested official documentation, asked follow-up questions, and required the team to verify the result. This approach sometimes took more time initially, but it helped me understand the root cause of problems and retain the lesson after solving them independently.

**3. Relevance to my academic background**

The project was relevant to my studies because it applied programming, database, networking, and embedded-system knowledge. Most of my previous projects ran only on a local computer. This project required me to consider user-facing deployment, traffic security, reliability, monitoring, backup, and documentation that would allow another person to operate the system.

Working with AWS strengthened my programming and database knowledge while introducing me to cloud infrastructure, DevOps, and system operations. I also became less hesitant about working with an architecture containing many components because I learned how to break a problem into layers and validate each part separately.

**4. Learning and skill-development opportunities**

In addition to technical knowledge, I learned how to work with teammates who had different strengths. Responsibilities were divided across backend, frontend, AWS infrastructure, hardware, and documentation, followed by coordination at integration points. Through integration and troubleshooting, I learned that progress updates, consistent data contracts, and change records are as important as completing each person's code.

I also had opportunities to hear practical experience about professional work, the use of AI, and career development. These discussions helped me identify missing skills and gave me a clearer direction for continuing to study cloud infrastructure, DevOps, and IoT.

**5. Community and teamwork**

The strongest quality I observed in the FCAJ community was a willingness to share. When a member encountered difficulty, people who had completed their tasks or had relevant experience usually offered support. Near the deadline, participants reviewed, commented on, and improved each other's work instead of focusing only on their individual responsibilities.

This environment helped me become more comfortable within the team and more confident when discussing problems. I also learned that teamwork is not limited to dividing tasks; it includes reporting blockers early, supporting integration work, and sharing responsibility for the final result.

---

### What I Valued Most

The part I valued most was the learning and knowledge-sharing spirit of the FCAJ community. People shared what they knew while still encouraging participants to research and develop independently. For me, FCAJ was not only a place to complete an internship project, but also an environment where I learned to work seriously, ask better questions, and support my teammates proactively.

### My Suggestions

- Establish a fixed working or review schedule so team members can arrange regular in-person meetings.
- Share the evaluation criteria and evidence checklist early so teams can prepare correctly from the beginning.
- Hold an integration review before the submission deadline to resolve issues across backend, frontend, infrastructure, and hardware.
- Continue the mentor approach of using questions and official documentation, supported by short progress checkpoints for each team.
